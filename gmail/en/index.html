Save Gmail attachments to Drive, automatically

Every attachment that lands in your Gmail is saved to a Google Drive folder for you. Three steps, about five minutes.

Before you start

FREE WHILE IN EARLY ACCESS

It is free right now. If you try it, a one-line note through "Feedback" (top right) would help a lot.

Copy the tool to my Drive

Click while signed in to Google and a copy prompt opens.

Saves automatically
Every 15 minutes, attachments from your Gmail are saved to the Drive folder you choose. Leave it alone and they pile up.
Renames files
The date goes in front, like "2026-09-11_invoice.pdf". You can also add the sender or the subject. Scans and faxes named "001.pdf" become easy to tell apart.
Files things away by month
Ten days after saving, a file moves into a month folder like "2026.09". Only recent files stay at the top, so the folder stays easy to browse. When the year changes, last year's months are grouped into a year folder.
Keeps a log
When, from whom, from which email, and what was saved: one row each in a spreadsheet. Click the subject to open the email, click the file name to open the file.
Never saves twice
The same email is never saved again. Files with the same name are numbered instead of overwritten. Emails you sent yourself and signature logos are skipped.
Lets you narrow it down
"Only from this sender", "only with this label": anything you can type into the Gmail search box works.
Stays inside your account
It runs entirely inside your own Google account. No email or file is ever sent to an outside server.

What you need

  • A Google account that uses Gmail
  • A computer for the setup (phones don't work for setup; once it's running you can forget about it)
  • If it's a company Google account, your admin must allow Apps Script (if it's blocked, step 2 will not go through)

Gmail only. Outlook and other mail services are not supported. Attachments must be attached to the email itself; Drive share links are not saved.

If you see "Google hasn't verified this app"

It appears during step 2. Nothing is broken. Every tool made by an individual shows this warning.

Click Advanced → Go to ... (unsafe) → Allow.

Then click "Allow" and you're done. The "developer" address shown is your own: once you made the copy, you became its owner. You are not handing access to anyone else.

The permission covers only your own Gmail and Drive. Nobody else can see your data.

What the questions mean

1

Where to save

Open the folder in Drive and paste its URL. Leave it empty and a new folder is created.

2

Which emails

Leave it empty for every email with an attachment.

3

How many days back

Empty means 30 days, and the first run saves all of them at once. To try with just a little, enter "1".

4

Days before filing away

After this many days a file moves into a month folder like "2026.09". When the year changes, last year's months go into a "2026" folder. Empty means 10.

5

How to name files

Empty means "date + original name".

The "📬 Attachment Saver" menu doesn't appear

You may have the original open instead of your copy. Go back to step 1.

If it still doesn't appear, the copy is still getting ready. Wait about 30 seconds and reload.

Nothing gets saved

There may be no email with an attachment in the period. Run "Set up" again and enter "30" for question 3.

Two files with the same name

Not a fault. When two emails carry attachments with the same name, a number is added so neither one is overwritten.

Other notes

Please don't sort the Log sheet. The month divider rows would end up in the wrong places. Filtering is fine.

Attachments in emails you sent yourself are skipped on purpose, so that forwarding an email does not save the same file twice.
